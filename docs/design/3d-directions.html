Gildra in 3D: six directions

What award-winning 3D sites do, and six ways Gildra could use 3D, animation and Clash's own characters. Every demo below is live: move your mouse or finger over it. They are rough sketches of the idea, built quickly; the real site would use polished models and art.

What the best 3D sites do

Awwwards Site of the Year 2024

Igloo Inc

One hero object (a 3D igloo) that changes as you scroll, custom ice and particle shaders, a camera journey to the footer. Built with Three.js + GSAP, and performance measured on low-end devices throughout.

Site of the Year 2023

Lusion

A cursor that physically moves the 3D scene, scroll-driven animation and a mobile version that keeps the feel without the heavy effects.

Game studios

Active Theory · Axolot Games

WebGL scenes you play with: Axolot (Site of the Day) lets visitors toss around 3D objects from its own games. The site becomes a small toy from the brand's world.

Riot Games

riotgames.com

Mostly cinematic key art, video and fast image delivery rather than heavy WebGL. The lesson: 3D is a signature moment, not the whole site.

Patterns in 2026's winners

  • One strong 3D concept per site, not a mix of effects.
  • Scroll tells the story (scrollytelling).
  • Still fast: Lighthouse mobile scores of 90+ are common among winners.
  • Reduced-motion settings are respected.
  • Generic particle backgrounds are overused now; custom shaders stand out.

Clash characters on stage

The official Fan Kit character renders are cut into depth layers (sky, village, characters, foreground) that shift with your pointer and scroll, like a moving poster. Heroes stride in on page load, gold dust drifts past them, and each section gets its own character.

Ideas
Barbarian King and Archer Queen flanking the hero ticket · a "champion" hero on each qualified team card · a Goblin on the "coming soon" page · the Grand Warden beside the rules
Rules
Only official Fan Kit art, never redrawn or modelled in 3D. Keep the "not official" disclaimer, and never suggest Supercell endorses the site.
Mobile
Fewer layers, tilt with phone motion; a still poster when reduced motion is on

How we'd build it so the site stays fast

Tools

  • React Three Fiber + drei for 3D inside Next.js
  • GSAP ScrollTrigger (already in the site) for scroll stories
  • Blender for the ticket, crests and map, exported as compressed glTF
  • Rive for small animated emblems and icons

Speed rules

  • A still image shows first, and the 3D loads after the page is usable
  • 3D only loads on pages that use it
  • Scenes pause when scrolled off screen
  • Target: Lighthouse 90+ on phones, same as today

Fallbacks

  • Reduced motion: a still, beautiful render
  • Low-power phones: lighter models or the still image
  • No WebGL: the image version, never a blank box

My recommendation

Combine 1 + 2 + 5 + 6: the 3D Golden Ticket as the one hero moment with Clash heroes standing beside it in layered depth, holo crest cards across the Teams pages, and gilded 3D numbers as key art where no character fits. That follows the award-winner rule of one strong 3D concept, keeps the site fast, and works well on phones.

Add 3 and 4 later for special moments: the War Table map for Road to Worlds once there's time to design a proper 3D map, and the gold dust for qualification moments.
